Case study 1 — P3 label-metadata mismatch and behavioral retest

T4/T5 failed twice, hours apart and well past Microsoft's documented 4-hour propagation window, with zero audit matches for P3 across the lab. SharePoint REST inspection (_api/web/lists/.../FieldValuesAsText) showed that _IpLabelId was empty on both documents, even though Word Online displayed the sensitivity labels.

Observed sequence: the Office interface displayed the labels; SharePoint showed empty label metadata; reapplying the labels populated that metadata; and both behavioral retests then passed.

Reopening each document in Word Online and explicitly selecting its label again populated _IpLabelId within about one minute. Both behavioral retests then passed. These results point to a label-metadata synchronization failure rather than a DLP rule-configuration problem; the exact underlying mechanism was not independently confirmed.

T4 post-fix — Copilot refuses to read the file and names the sensitivity label + DLP policy as the reason.
T5 post-fix — identical outcome for the Research-Plan document.

Case study 2 — Delayed audit events initially showed no P2 matches

An 18:17 ET audit recheck found zero matches for P2 and was correctly logged as INCONCLUSIVE rather than a false PASS/FAIL. A fresh query the next morning — with no change to the policy or the test — found "DLP rule matched" events aligned almost to the minute with both T2 test runs. The earlier recheck simply predated Activity Explorer's ingestion of those events.

Activity Explorer, filtered to DLP rule matched — confirms P2 fired on EMusk, correcting the earlier reading.
Takeaway: No matches in an audit query does not, by itself, show that a policy failed to run. In this lab, the matching events appeared in a later query.
