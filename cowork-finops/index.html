Cowork FinOps Quickstart

Generate a rate-based, FOCUS-aligned view of Microsoft 365 Copilot Credit consumption (branded "Cowork" in these community templates) from two tenant exports, using Microsoft's public Analytics Hub — Cowork Billing browser tool. Originally written October 28, 2026; revised after independent technical review on the same day.

Audience: Microsoft 365 administrators, FinOps practitioners, and finance analysts who already have (or can request) Copilot Cost Management and Entra export access.
Prerequisites: a supported admin role for each export (see Step 02), an agreed reporting period, and an agreed allocation rule before you circulate any output as a real chargeback.

This is an independent companion walkthrough, not official Microsoft documentation. It documents a specific community-built browser tool and two public GitHub Power BI templates linked from Microsoft's Analytics Hub page; it is not a Microsoft support article and carries no Microsoft endorsement of accuracy.

Suitable for: consumption analysis, showback, allocation modeling, and pilot/internal review.
Not sufficient by itself for: invoice reconciliation, general-ledger posting, or a disputed chargeback — those require your actual Microsoft billing record.

Privacy: the tool is designed to process your CSVs entirely in the browser — no upload, no backend. In the run documented here, the CSV contents were not transmitted in observed network requests (verified via DevTools → Network). That is a spot-check, not a code audit: for sensitive or regulated data, review the published source yourself and confirm it meets your organization's data-handling requirements before loading identifiable production data. Prefer synthetic or approved data for evaluation runs.

How to read the numbers

The report borrows FOCUS (FinOps Open Cost & Usage Specification) vocabulary — List, Contracted, Effective, and Billed cost — but it computes those columns from credits × a rate you configure, not from your invoice. Keep these three concepts separate:

TermWhat it actually isSafe to use for
Consumed creditsOperational usage pulled straight from the Copilot Cost Management exportAdoption, policy, and usage trend analysis
Modeled cost (shown as "List/Contracted/Effective" cost)Credits × the list or contracted unit price you type into the rate fieldsPlanning and illustrative allocation — not a certified FOCUS dataset
Estimated billed costThe report's "Billed cost" figure — still a modeled estimate, not pulled from your invoiceDirectional planning only; reconcile against your actual Microsoft invoice before using it financially

The "FOCUS v1.2 columns" badge on the report means the column labels follow FOCUS terminology, not that the dataset is a certified, fully conformant FOCUS export (it doesn't model prepaid-commitment amortization or shared-cost allocation). Treat every dollar figure on the page as a planning estimate until reconciled against your bill.

01Start at the Cowork Billing hub

Everything lives off one page: microsoft.github.io/Analytics-Hub/cowork-billing. It lists the Power BI templates (Consumption Central, Cowork Chargeback) and the no-install browser tools (Chargeback, FinOps & FOCUS, ROI Model, Policy Helper).

This walkthrough uses the FinOps browser tool first (Steps 02–06); the Power BI templates are covered in Step 07 once you know what data they need.

02Confirm your export format, then pull your two files

The FinOps tool expects exactly these two exports — and specifically the Copilot Cost Management consumption export, not a different Copilot usage report (Microsoft also ships other Copilot usage views, such as the Viva Insights consumption/AI cost dashboard, with their own schemas; don't substitute one for the other without checking column names first).

ExportWhereTypical role requiredGives you
Entra usersEntra admin center → Identity → Users → All users → Download usersA directory role with user read access, e.g. User Administrator, Reports Reader, or Global Reader — confirm with your Entra admin if the download option isn't available to youDisplay name and UPN plus whichever organizational attributes your tenant's export includes
Copilot credit usageMicrosoft 365 admin center → Copilot → Cost management → Consumption → Export CSVA reader-based role such as AI Reader, Global Reader, or License Administrator (per Microsoft's current Cost Management role guidance)One row per user: credits used, credit limit, license status, last activity, session count

Not every organizational attribute is guaranteed. Display name, UPN, job title, department, and country are typically present in a standard Entra users export. Cost Center, Business Unit, Job Family, and Manager may need additional attribute selection in the export experience or enrichment from an HR/org system — verify those columns exist and are populated before you rely on them for allocation.

Column names are auto-detected by the tool, so most layouts of this specific export work unmodified. Open both files in Excel first to sanity-check row counts and confirm the expected columns are present before loading them.

03Load both files into the FinOps report

Open the tool and drop each file in its own zone — Entra export on the left, Copilot credit export on the right. The Generate FinOps Report button stays disabled until both are loaded.

There's also a Fallback credit limit field. It only applies when your credit export has no per-user limit column — which happens with older export layouts or tenants still on earlier consumption-reporting formats that predate the per-user limit column. Leave the default unless you know your tenant's actual policy limit differs; if your export does include a limit column, this field is ignored.

Large tenants: this tool parses both CSVs entirely in your browser's memory. For tenants in the tens of thousands of users (a rough guide, not a hard cutoff), the page may become slow or unresponsive. If that happens, skip ahead to the Power BI templates in Step 07, which are built for larger, refreshable datasets.

04Click Generate and read the report

The two files join on User Principal Name — that's how this tool matches records, not a platform-wide identity standard — and the page renders a FOCUS-aligned modeled cost view: scope strip, live rate controls (List vs. Contracted unit price), KPI band, cost-allocation cut (department / cost center / business unit, searchable and filterable), rate-comparison bars, and a methodology/glossary section.

Read the overage notice on the page. Microsoft's current documentation states that qualifying per-user overage from a running task can, at Microsoft's sole discretion, be excluded from policy-limit consumption, not billed, and not shown as consumed credits in Cost Management — this is current behavior, not a contractual guarantee, and the report cannot reconstruct unrecorded overage from a credit export. The policy-level spending limit is the governing budget control for a given spending policy, but don't present it to Finance as an invoice-grade hard ceiling — in-flight work and enforcement timing mean dashboard consumption doesn't map 1:1 to billed usage. Always reconcile the report's "Billed cost" figure against your actual Microsoft invoice before treating it as a financial number.

05Validate before you trust the report

Before you circulate any allocation or chargeback figure, check these in the on-screen report and your source files:

  • Confirm the reporting period the credit export actually covers, and whether your read of it is monthly or cumulative.
  • Record total usage rows and distinct people in the source export before comparing to what the tool shows.
  • Look for unmatched identities — UPNs present in one file but not the other won't be allocated correctly.
  • Check for blank Department / Cost Center / Business Unit values; those rows fall outside your allocation cut.
  • Watch for duplicate rows if your credit export spans multiple services or dates.
  • If anyone changed security groups or spending policy mid-period, confirm their usage is still attributed the way you expect.
  • Reconcile total imported credits in the tool against the total in your source CSV before trusting any allocation.
  • Don't post a chargeback until unmatched and unallocated usage has an agreed treatment (exclude, re-assign, or flag).

06Export the artifacts you need

The toolbar above the report body has five actions: one to load a different dataset, and four exports.

Because the PDF captures the live report at 2x scale as an image, the resulting file can be large (tens to 100+ MB for a report with a long allocation table) and its text isn't selectable. If you need numbers for analysis, use the two CSV exports instead; if you need a smaller, text-searchable PDF for sharing, your browser's own Print → Save as PDF on this page will generally produce a much lighter file.

07Optional: go deeper in Power BI

The browser tool is a fast first read and a single snapshot. For recurring, refreshable reporting, open the Power BI template that matches your goal — the two template families expect different inputs, so don't assume one set of CSVs works for both:

  • Cowork Chargeback (GitHub) — use for departmental showback/chargeback: billing-model comparison (pay-as-you-go, prepaid, hybrid), prepaid pool allocation, per-user watchlist, row-level security, FOCUS cost view. Its Cowork Chargeback - CSV.pbit variant reads the same Entra + Copilot credit exports from Step 02; its Cowork Chargeback - Direct Query.pbit variant connects live to Viva Insights instead and needs no CSVs.
  • Consumption Central (GitHub) — use for a cross-product view spanning Cowork/Work IQ, Copilot Studio, GitHub Copilot, and Azure AI Foundry. It uses its own data-source setup (local CSV, Fabric, or live Viva Direct Query, per its 1. Local CSV / 2. Fabric / 3. Viva Direct folders) — follow that repo's own README/data-sources instructions rather than assuming the Step 02 exports are sufficient on their own; it ships with synthetic sample data so you can review the full report before pointing it at a tenant.

Notes & methodology

  • FOCUS-aligned terminology: List / Contracted / Effective / Billed cost labels, same vocabulary as the sibling Chargeback tool — see "How to read the numbers" above before treating any of these as invoice-grade.
  • Showback, as this tool implements it: the full effective cost of consumption attributed to a unit, with no financial transfer. Chargeback, as this tool implements it: over-allowance credits × contracted rate — one possible organizational allocation policy, not a Microsoft billing definition or the only valid chargeback method. Other models (full consumption, fixed allocation, blended) are equally valid; this tool only computes the overage-basis one.
  • Single-period snapshot — no amortization, forecasting, or anomaly detection in the browser tool (that's what the Power BI templates add).
  • Both CSV exports are required; the tool will not generate a report from Entra data alone or credit data alone.
  • Questions or feedback on the methodology: copilot-roi-advisory-team-gh@microsoft.com (Microsoft's Copilot ROI Advisory team, who own the upstream Analytics Hub tool — not the author of this companion page).
