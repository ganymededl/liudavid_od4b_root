Choosing the right AI for the job

Copilot, Cowork, or agents?

Microsoft gives you more than one way to work with AI. Use Copilot for fast help in the apps you already use. Use Cowork to hand off a defined, multi-step task and refine the result. Use Copilot agents to embed AI into a repeatable process for a team or your customers. The lanes overlap — this guide helps you pick a good starting point.

What each one is

Three ways to put AI to work

The same request can often be handled by more than one. Start with the lane that matches how much you want to delegate and how repeatable the work is.

Interactive assistance

Microsoft 365 Copilot

Copilot helps you do the work, in the flow of your apps.

  • Great for Q&A, drafting, summarizing, analysis, and ideation
  • Works inside Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams, PowerPoint, and Copilot Chat
  • Best for one-turn or short back-and-forth tasks
  • You stay the operator — you ask, review, and take the next step
Choose it when: you want an answer or an assist right now — not a hands-off workstream.
Bounded delegated execution

Microsoft Cowork

Cowork runs a defined, multi-step task with you in the loop.

  • Give it one bounded goal; it plans steps and works through them
  • Strong for batch production — one output per account, recipient, or item
  • Collects finished files in an output folder to preview or download
  • Pauses for your approval before sensitive actions
Choose it when: the outcome is defined, you want it delegated, and you'll still review and refine.
Custom & autonomous

Copilot agents

Purpose-built agents that run a process and can be shared.

  • Build with Copilot Studio or use prebuilt agents
  • Ground answers in your own data, documents, and systems
  • Automate a workflow and trigger it on a schedule or an event
  • Deploy to a team, the whole org, or customer-facing channels
Choose it when: the work is repeatable, needs your data, or should serve many people the same way.
These are lanes, not hard gates. Copilot fits fast assistance; Cowork fits bounded, delegated execution with review; agents fit repeatable, grounded, or shared capabilities. Familiarity, licensing, and how much you want to delegate also matter — many tasks work well in more than one.

They work together

Handing work off between them

The best results often move across lanes as the work matures.

Copilot → Cowork

You explored an idea and shaped the output in Copilot. When it becomes a defined, repeatable deliverable across many items, hand it to Cowork to produce at scale.

Cowork → Agents

A Cowork task you run every week is a signal. When the process is stable and should serve others, turn it into a Copilot agent that runs on its own.

Agents → Copilot

An agent surfaces an answer or draft; you refine the specifics with Copilot in your app before it goes out.

Start simple, escalate on need

Begin in the lightest lane that can do the job. Move up only when scale, repeatability, or reach makes it worth it.

Trust & control

Enterprise data protection, by design

All three run on Microsoft's enterprise foundation. A few points customers ask about most.

Your data stays yours

Prompts, responses, and your organization's data are not used to train the foundation models. Content is protected within your Microsoft 365 tenant boundary.

Permissions are honored

AI only surfaces content a user already has access to. Existing identity, access, and sensitivity labels are respected end to end.

Admin & governance controls

IT manages availability, data connections, and agent publishing through the Microsoft 365 admin and Copilot Studio governance surfaces.

Compliance & residency

Backed by Microsoft's enterprise compliance commitments, with data-residency options depending on service and region.
