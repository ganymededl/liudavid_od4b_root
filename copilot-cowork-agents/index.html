Choose the right way to work with AI.

Use Copilot for simple tasks and fast chats in the flow of Microsoft 365. Use Cowork for bounded execution when you want to delegate a defined task, stay in the loop, and refine the result through one contained workstream. Use Copilot agents for repeatable, grounded work you build once and deploy to a team or the people you serve. The boundaries overlap, and the best starting point can vary by user.

Definitions

What each name means here

“Copilot” is a broad product family. To make this guide useful, Copilot below means Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat and in-app assistance—not Cowork, agents, or every product carrying the Copilot name.

Interactive assistance

Copilot helps you do the work.

You remain the operator. You ask, inspect the response, and choose the next step.

  • Best for one-turn or short back-and-forth tasks
  • Works well for Q&A, summaries, drafts, analysis, and ideation
  • Useful inside Word, Outlook, Teams, PowerPoint, Excel, and Copilot Chat
Choose it when: you want an answer or assist—not an autonomous workstream.
Bounded delegated execution

Cowork runs a defined workstream with you in the loop.

You give Cowork one bounded task in one chat. It plans steps, acquires skills, responds to refinement, and collects finished artifacts in an output folder.

  • Best when the outcome is defined but review may still improve it
  • Strong for batch production: one output per account, recipient, or item
  • Keeps inputs, outputs, skills, and progress in one task panel
  • Pauses for approval before sensitive actions
Choose it when: the work is bounded, delegation matters, and you want organized outputs with visible progress.
Custom & autonomous

Copilot agents run a repeatable process you can share.

Purpose-built assistants that ground on your data, take actions through connectors, and run for a team or the people you serve. Build with Copilot Studio or use prebuilt agents.

  • Ground answers in your documents, data, and systems
  • Automate a workflow and trigger it on a schedule or event
  • Publish to Teams, your website, or Microsoft 365 Copilot
  • Govern, secure, and monitor centrally
Choose it when: the work is repeatable, needs your data, or should serve many people the same way.
These are lanes, not hard gates: Copilot fits simple assistance and fast conversation; Cowork fits bounded delegated execution with review and refinement; agents fit repeatable, grounded, or shared work. All three overlap, so familiarity, licensing, and comfort with delegation also matter.
What's documented

What Microsoft documents confirm

These first-party facts establish the official product boundaries. The rest of this guide explains how the capabilities behave in real work.

Copilot Chat · documented

Cloud conversation for fast, focused support

Microsoft positions Copilot Chat for drafting, summarizing, answering questions, generating content, and focused single-task support. The human acts on the output.

Official distinction: Chat generates content and insights; Cowork completes coordinated actions across Microsoft 365.
Cowork · documented

Contained multi-step execution across Microsoft 365

Cowork plans steps, acquires skills, creates or manages Microsoft 365 content, and pauses for approval before sensitive actions—minutes-to-hours execution for end-to-end work across apps.

Containment is native: a side panel shows progress, input folder, output folder, loaded skills, schedules, and permissions.
Copilot agents · documented

Custom agents grounded in your data

Copilot Studio lets makers build agents that plan steps, ground on your knowledge sources, take actions through connectors, and run autonomously or on triggers. Prebuilt agents are also available across Microsoft 365.

Governed by design: makers and admins manage knowledge, actions, publishing channels, and security centrally.

Agent power

Where Copilot agents extend furthest

Cowork is optimized for contained execution. Copilot agents are an extensible platform for capabilities you build, connect, automate, and deploy beyond a single task run.

1Grounding on your data

Connect agents to SharePoint, Dataverse, websites, and enterprise systems so answers cite your knowledge.

2Actions & connectors

Take real actions through 1,000+ connectors and custom APIs, with approvals where needed.

3Autonomous triggers

Run on a schedule or when an event fires—new email, record change, or form submission.

4Multi-agent orchestration

Compose agents that hand off to each other for research, drafting, and review.

5Custom topics & logic

Design conversation flows, business rules, and variables for reliable, repeatable outcomes.

6Channels & reach

Publish to Teams, your website, Microsoft 365 Copilot, or customer-facing surfaces.

7Governance & lifecycle

Manage, secure, and monitor agents centrally with maker and admin controls.

8Extensibility

Extend with Power Platform, Azure, plugins, and pro-code when the need is bigger.

What agents can become

More than a chat or task runner

Agents can power self-service for the people you serve, staff help desks, records and status lookup, document and form intake, knowledge search, scheduled digests, and end-to-end automated workflows—each running the same way, every time.

Why not always an agent?

Building an agent is an investment

An agent needs design, grounding, testing, and governance. When the work is a one-off, or a bounded task that just needs to run once and return a clean output folder, Copilot or Cowork is the better experience.

Usage patterns

How they show up in real work

The clearest signal is the shape of the work—who steers, how long it runs, where it executes, and whether it repeats.

Copilot pattern

A question → an answer you act on

You stay in the app, ask for a summary, draft, or analysis, review it, and take the next step yourself.

Signal: the need is fast, conversational, and app-local.
Cowork pattern

One contained task → a folder of finished outputs

You define the deliverable, delegate it, and refine within one task; the results land together, ready to review or download.

Signal: the outcome is knowable and the work fans out into artifacts.
Agent pattern

A defined process → run repeatedly, grounded and governed

You build the capability once, ground it on your data, publish it, and let it serve many people on a schedule or trigger.

Signal: the same work should run consistently for a team or the people you serve.

Handoffs

The tools can hand work to each other

Choosing a starting point does not lock the whole task to one surface. Move when the shape of the work changes.

Copilot→Cowork

The task grew beyond a fast chat and now has a defined outcome that can be delegated, reviewed, and refined as one contained workstream.

Cowork→Agents

A Cowork task you run every week is a signal. When the process is stable and should serve others, turn it into an agent that runs on its own.

Agents→Copilot

An agent surfaces an answer or draft; you refine the specifics with Copilot in your app before it goes out.

Start simple→Escalate on need

Begin in the lightest lane that can do the job. Move up only when scale, repeatability, or reach makes it worth it.

How to ask each tool

The same topic is framed differently depending on whether you want assistance, delegation, or a running capability.

Copilot prompt

Ask for the next useful response

Summarize this email thread from a faculty member. List the decisions, open questions, and owners, then draft a concise reply for me to review.
Cowork work order

Define the finished outcome

Run outreach for the approved admitted-student list and message. Create one personalized draft per student, don't send anything, and save every draft plus a summary in one output folder for my review.
Agent brief

Name the capability and controls

Build a student-support agent grounded in our admissions and registrar help content. Answer questions, cite sources, escalate to staff on low confidence, keep student records read-only, and follow FERPA.
Guardrails

Guardrails apply everywhere

No tool choice removes human accountability. Access, review, privacy, and approval requirements still follow the work.

1Use only approved data

Do not paste or connect information you are not authorized to use in that experience.

2State the boundary

Name allowed sources, date ranges, output location, sensitivity, and what the tool must not touch.

3Review consequential actions

Sending, publishing, scheduling, deleting, purchasing, or updating a system needs the right human approval.

4Verify the result

Check facts, numbers, recipients, permissions, citations, and business logic before relying on the output.

Important: never choose a more powerful surface to bypass a control that applies in Copilot. Use the least-powerful surface that can complete the work safely and effectively. All three run on Microsoft's enterprise foundation—prompts, responses, and your data are not used to train the foundation models, and existing identity, permissions, and sensitivity labels are respected.
