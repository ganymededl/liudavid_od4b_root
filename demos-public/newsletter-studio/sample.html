SUCCESS STORY

Small automation. Outsized impact.

Cedar Valley County (fictional)

CSAM: Taylor Morgan · CSA: Alex Rivera

THE OPPORTUNITY

A lean county IT team was spending hours each week manually reviewing service requests, leaving less time for work that directly supports residents.

THE APPROACH

The CSU team helped build an Azure-based workflow that classifies requests, routes them to the right owner, and surfaces a simple operational dashboard. A focused pilot proved the approach before a wider rollout.

THE IMPACT

  • Reduced manual triage time by 40% in the pilot.
  • Gave service owners a shared view of request status.
  • Created a repeatable blueprint for two additional departments.

IN CONVERSATION

The human side of customer success

Beyond the Blueprint · Part 01

In conversation with Avery Brooks · Interview by Morgan Ellis

Behind every successful deployment is a conversation that changed the direction. In our new interview series, we’re meeting the people who make those conversations count.

Q.What’s the first question you ask a new customer?
A.What would a genuinely better day look like for your team? It helps us move beyond a product conversation and understand the work that really matters.
Q.How do you turn that answer into a plan?
A.We choose one outcome, agree on how we’ll measure it, and build a small proof point together. Progress creates trust much faster than a long roadmap.
Q.What advice would you share with a new teammate?
A.Stay curious a little longer. The most useful insight often comes just after the question you almost didn’t ask.

“Progress creates trust much faster than a long roadmap.”

TIP OF THE MONTH

Make your next Copilot prompt count

A great prompt is a clear brief. Give Copilot the context, the goal, and the shape of a useful answer — then make it a conversation.

  1. Start with the outcome: explain what you’re trying to accomplish and who it’s for.
  2. Add the right context: reference approved files or meeting notes you’re authorized to use.
  3. Specify the format: ask for a short table, an action list, or a customer-ready summary.
  4. Review and refine: verify facts and citations before sharing.

TRY THIS PROMPT

Using my selected meeting notes, draft a follow-up email with three agreed next steps, an owner for each, and any open questions. Flag missing details rather than guessing.

 

TRY THIS PROMPT

Help me prepare for a customer success review. From the documents I select, summarize progress against our goals and suggest three questions to explore next.
